# Ahmad Moeineddin

**Computational Mechanics & Scientific Computing**

I am a computational mechanics researcher and engineer working on nonlinear
continuum mechanics, constitutive modeling, large-deformation simulation,
high-performance scientific computing, and scientific machine learning.

My current research at TU Dresden spans three main directions:

### Computational Snow Mechanics & Tire–Snow Interaction
Development, calibration, and validation of finite-strain constitutive models
for snow, together with large-deformation simulation of tire–snow interaction
within an industrial research collaboration with Continental AG.

### Scalable Material Point Method
Development of GPU and multi-GPU Material Point Method frameworks for
large-scale simulations, including parallel domain decomposition,
performance optimization, moving computational domains, and scalable
large-deformation analysis.

### Physics-Informed Computational Mechanics
Scientific machine learning for forward and inverse problems in mechanics,
including Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs), material parameter
identification, nonlinear constitutive modeling, biomechanical applications,
and finite operator learning.
